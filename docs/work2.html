Work

Research, collaboration, and field-based methods focused on Indigenous stewardship, conservation, and ecology in the Amazon.

Work

My work focuses on how Indigenous stewardship sustains ecological and cultural systems in the Amazon. Across collaborative research, ecological field methods, and spatial analysis, I work with Indigenous communities and partners to better understand territorial governance, biodiversity, and biocultural resilience.

Rather than treating conservation as only a technical problem, my research asks how care, reciprocity, governance, and long-term relationships with place shape more durable forms of stewardship.

This page highlights selected themes and projects. For a full list of publications, see Publications.

Territorial Resilience

Indigenous stewardship and biocultural systems in the Amazon

Much of my current work examines how Indigenous peoples collectively sustain biocultural processes and territorial resilience across Amazonian territories. As a Co-Principal Investigator on a National Science Foundation Dynamics of Integrated Socio-Environmental Systems (DISES) grant, I collaborate with Cofán, Siekopai, and Siona communities to investigate stewardship strategies and their role in sustaining both ecological and cultural continuity.

This work combines transdisciplinary research, collaborative field methods, and capacity-building processes that support Indigenous researchers in generating, interpreting, and applying findings in ways that strengthen self-determined stewardship.

Selected contributions - Collaborative research with Cofán, Siekopai, and Siona communities - Co-development of culturally grounded research tools - Support for Indigenous-led data analysis and interpretation - Research on governance, reciprocity, and territorial continuity

Conservation and Care

Care, reciprocity, and governance as conservation practice

This area of my work focuses on how Indigenous practices of care, reciprocity, and governance sustain ecological abundance, cultural vitality, and territorial integrity in Amazonia. Through long-term collaboration with Cofán communities and broader regional analyses of Indigenous territories, I explore how Indigenous stewardship contributes not only to biodiversity conservation, but also to more equitable and grounded models of conservation practice.

This research is particularly interested in the relationship between everyday forms of care and larger territorial outcomes: how hunting norms, collective governance, and reciprocal relations with forests, animals, and plants support resilient socioecological systems.

Selected contributions - Research on Indigenous conservation and stewardship - Analysis of reciprocity, governance, and territorial care - Work connecting local practices to broader conservation debates - Publications bridging ecology, political ecology, and conservation science

Ecology and Natural History

Species, landscapes, and more-than-human relations

Through collaborative research with Indigenous communities, I study the diversity of life across Amazonian territories, including species distributions, ecological dynamics, and cultural relationships with animals. This work contributes to biodiversity science while also engaging Indigenous knowledge systems as critical ways of observing, classifying, and understanding ecological change.

Some of this work takes the form of formal academic publication; some of it takes the form of community-authored field guides and locally grounded documentation that supports education, territorial governance, and intergenerational knowledge transmission.

Selected contributions - Species inventories and natural history documentation - Research on animal distributions and habitat use - Collaborative field guides with Indigenous communities - Work linking biodiversity science with Indigenous knowledge

Field Methods and Tools

Methods for research in complex forest environments

As an interdisciplinary researcher, I combine ecological monitoring with participatory and community-based methods to better understand and support conservation in complex environments. My methodological work includes developing tools for estimating animal populations in dense forests, collaborating with Indigenous trackers and hunters, and using methods such as camera trapping, participatory mapping, and spatial analysis to address practical research challenges.

This dimension of my work is especially focused on methods that are rigorous but also workable in real field conditions, where visibility is limited, logistics are difficult, and local knowledge is essential.

Selected contributions - Density estimation from track counts - Distance sampling in dense tropical forest - Camera trapping and participatory ecological monitoring - Spatial analysis and applied field methods
